# Online vs In-Person Tutoring: Which Works Better?

"Is online tutoring as effective as in-person?" is one of the most common questions parents search before choosing a program.

The short answer: **format matters less than design.** A well-run online session beats a poorly run in-person one, and the reverse is also true. But for some children and some gaps, format does make a real difference.

## The real problem behind the question

Parents usually weigh convenience, drive time and cost. The question underneath is: *will my child actually learn in this setting?* That depends on three things:

1. **Can your child stay engaged without someone physically next to them?**
2. **Does the gap require watching them work** — handwriting math steps, tracking a line of text, catching a repeated error in real time?
3. **Will the format be consistent** week after week?

## When in-person tends to work better

- **Younger children (roughly grades 1–4)** who need hands-on guidance and help staying on task.
- **Math gaps where the tutor must see the work** — dropped negatives, misaligned steps, the same mistake pattern repeated.
- **Early reading**, where a tutor tracks what the child is looking at and saying.
- **Kids who drift on screens** or multitask with other tabs.
- **Students who need a "school-like" setting** to take work seriously.

## When online tends to work well

- **Older, self-directed students** (often middle and high school).
- **Targeted practice or test prep** with a clear plan, such as SAT sections.
- **Families with tight schedules** where drive time would mean fewer sessions.
- **Continuity** during travel or busy seasons.

## What research on tutoring emphasizes

Research on effective tutoring focuses on **design**: small groups, sessions three or more times a week, the same tutor over time, and instruction guided by the student's actual errors (Susanna Loeb, Stanford, 2022). Choose the format that lets your child get **those** conditions consistently.

## A quick decision guide

| If your child… | Lean toward |
|---|---|
| Is in early elementary | In-person |
| Keeps repeating the same math error | In-person (at least to start) |
| Is a focused teen with a clear goal | Online works well |
| Loses focus on screens | In-person |
| Can only do 1 session a week in person but 3 online | Consider online for frequency |

Many families use a **hybrid**: start in person to diagnose and correct patterns, then continue online for practice.

## How most programs miss this

Some online programs are video homework help; some in-person centers are supervised worksheet rooms. In both cases the format hides the same issue — no one is diagnosing and correcting the real blocker.

## FAQ

**Is online tutoring as effective as in-person?**
It can be, especially for older, focused students with a clear goal. For younger children and gaps that require watching the child work, in-person is often more effective.

**Is online tutoring worth it?**
Yes when it's structured — a diagnostic, a clear target, consistent tutors and regular sessions. Less so when it's unstructured homework help.

**What age is best for online tutoring?**
Many middle and high school students do well online. Younger children usually benefit more from in-person support.

**Is in-person tutoring better for math?**
Often, when a child has a repeated mistake pattern. The tutor can watch each step and correct the error as it happens.

**Can we combine online and in-person tutoring?**
Yes. A common approach is in-person to diagnose and correct, then online for continued practice.
