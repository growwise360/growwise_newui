# Is Tutoring Worth It?

"Is tutoring worth it?" is one of the most-searched questions parents ask before paying for help. The honest answer: **tutoring can be one of the most effective things you do for your child — or an expensive weekly homework session.** The difference is the design, not the word "tutoring."

## The real problem behind the question

Most parents asking this have already seen one of two things:

- Tutoring that helped with tonight's homework but didn't change test scores.
- A child who keeps making the same mistakes no matter how much practice they do.

Both point to the same issue: help without diagnosis. If no one finds the real blocker, sessions fill with more worksheets on things the child already knows — or skip the thing they don't.

## What research shows

Education economist **Susanna Loeb**, reviewing the evidence on tutoring (Stanford, 2022), found that **well-designed, high-impact tutoring can help students who are behind catch up by half a school year to a full school year** — larger than many other school interventions.

The features that made the difference:

- **Small groups** — no more than about three students per tutor
- **Frequency** — at least 30 minutes, three or more times a week
- **Consistency** — the same tutor over time
- **Data-informed** — instruction guided by what the student actually gets wrong

Loeb also noted the flip side: less structured, drop-in homework help **often shows much smaller results**.

## The real disadvantages of tutoring

Parents search for this too, so here it is plainly:

- **Cost without a plan.** Paying for sessions with no diagnosis or goal.
- **Dependence.** A child who only works with an adult next to them.
- **Homework-only help.** Grades hold, but understanding doesn't grow.
- **Mismatch.** A tutor strong in the subject but not in finding learning gaps.
- **Time.** Another commitment in a busy week — worth it only if it produces change.

Each of these is avoidable with the right program design.

## Signs tutoring is likely worth it for your child

- The same **mistake pattern** repeats across homework and tests.
- Grades are fine, but your child needs constant help at home.
- Test scores are lower than homework scores.
- A teacher mentions a skill gap.
- Your child avoids starting work in one subject.

## How most programs miss this

Many programs start with the homework folder or a fixed curriculum. Neither answers the first question: *what exactly is my child missing?*

## Why GrowWise?

1. **Diagnose** — find the real blocker (concept, mistake pattern, reading with evidence, or writing clarity).
2. **Target** — build a plan around that gap, not around generic grade-level worksheets.
3. **Practice with correction** — the student fixes the pattern and proves it on new problems.
4. **Show progress** — parents get clear updates on what changed.

## What to look for in any tutoring program

- Does it start with a real diagnostic?
- Can it name your child's specific gap after the first session?
- Is the group small enough for individual correction?
- Does your child work independently, not just alongside the tutor?
- How will you know it's working — and when?

## FAQ

**Is tutoring worth the money?**
It is when it's structured: a diagnostic, a clear target, small groups or 1:1, regular sessions, and consistent tutors. Unstructured homework help often shows much smaller results.

**What are the disadvantages of tutoring?**
Cost without a plan, dependence on the tutor, help that only covers homework, and a poor match between tutor and need. A diagnostic-first program avoids most of these.

**How do I know if my child needs a tutor?**
Look for repeated mistakes, a gap between homework and test scores, constant help needed at home, or avoidance of one subject.

**What is the best age to start tutoring?**
There's no single age. Start when a gap appears — early gaps in reading or math foundations are faster to close than gaps found years later.

**Will AI replace tutors?**
AI can explain steps and give practice. It can't reliably diagnose why your child keeps making the same mistake or hold them accountable to fix it. Many families use both.
