# Kumon vs Mathnasium vs RSM: Which Is Better?

When parents start looking for math help, three names come up again and again. Google's autocomplete shows how common the comparison is: "kumon vs mathnasium," "kumon vs rsm vs mathnasium," "mathnasium vs kumon which is better."

Each program is built on a different idea of how children learn math. None is "best" for every child. The right choice depends on **what your child is actually missing.**

*Descriptions below are based on each company's own published materials as of September 2026. Local centers can vary; confirm details with the center you're considering.*

## How each one works

| | **Kumon** | **Mathnasium** | **Russian School of Math (RSM)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Core idea** | Individualized, self-directed worksheets in very small steps | Assessment-based custom learning plan focused on number sense | Continuous K–12 curriculum focused on deep conceptual understanding |
| **How it starts** | Placement at a comfortable level, regardless of grade | Verbal and written assessment | Free ~30-minute evaluation for class placement |
| **Format** | Independent worksheet practice with instructor observation | Center sessions, typically twice a week for an hour; online available | Group class once a week; average ~12 students; three levels per grade |
| **Homework / practice** | Daily worksheet practice | Varies by center | Homework designed to take about half the class time |
| **Tends to fit** | Kids who need fluency and routine, and can work independently | Kids who need foundations and number sense rebuilt | Kids who are ready for challenge and a structured, rigorous class |

## The real problem behind the question

Parents usually compare programs by method, schedule and price. But the most important question comes first: **what is the real blocker?**

- If your child **knows how but is slow or error-prone**, fluency practice may help.
- If your child **has a gap in a foundation** (fractions, negatives, place value), they need that specific gap found and closed.
- If your child **is solid and bored**, they may need challenge, not remediation.
- If your child **keeps making the same mistake**, they need the **mistake pattern** corrected — more repetition of the same error won't fix it.

A program that's great for one of these can be the wrong fit for another.

## How most programs miss this

Any program can miss a child's real gap if placement is too broad or practice is too generic. Common results parents describe:

- Worksheets the child already knows, done faster, without new understanding.
- A class pace that's right for the group but not for your child's gap.
- Improvement in the program's own levels without matching improvement at school.

## FAQ

**Which is better, Kumon or Mathnasium?**
It depends on the child. Kumon centers on independent worksheet practice in small steps. Mathnasium builds a custom plan from an assessment and focuses on number sense. Choose based on whether your child needs fluency practice or foundation gaps closed.

**What is the difference between Kumon and Russian School of Math?**
Kumon is individualized, self-paced worksheet practice. RSM is a weekly group class with a structured K–12 curriculum and homework, focused on conceptual depth.

**Is RSM harder than Kumon?**
RSM classes are designed around challenging, conceptual problems in a group setting. Kumon moves in small steps and can advance beyond grade level. "Harder" depends on your child's level and learning style.

**Is Mathnasium better than a private tutor?**
Mathnasium follows a structured method across centers. A private tutor can be more flexible but quality varies. Either works best when it starts with a real diagnostic of your child's gap.

**Which math program is best for a child who keeps making the same mistakes?**
One that identifies the specific mistake pattern and has the child correct it on new problems — not one that only adds more practice of the same kind.
