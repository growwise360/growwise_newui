# Can Your Child Explain It Without AI? A 5-Minute Reasoning Check

Many parents see finished homework and assume the learning happened. In 2026, that assumption needs a second look.

A Common Sense Media survey of 1,204 young people ages 9–17 (March 2026) found that **85% of kids who use AI have used it for schoolwork**, about half use it weekly, and **nearly 1 in 4 would ask an AI chatbot before a trusted adult** for homework help. A separate Common Sense Media survey of teens (April–May 2026) found **63% go straight to a chatbot for answers**, and **39% say they feel they "miss out on learning"** when AI completes assignments.

The homework looks done. The question is whether the thinking got done.

## The real problem behind the symptom

AI is not the problem by itself. The problem is **answer without reasoning**.

A child can submit a correct math solution or a clean paragraph and still be unable to:

- say why a step works
- redo a similar problem with different numbers
- find the evidence in a passage that supports a claim
- explain their own sentence out loud

That gap stays invisible until a test, where there is no chatbot. This is why many parents are surprised when homework grades are fine but test scores drop.

## What the research and patterns show

- A January 2026 Brookings Institution Center for Universal Education report, based on hundreds of interviews and a review of 400+ studies, warned of students slipping into "passenger mode" — present, but not doing the thinking. One educator quoted in coverage of the report said: "Students can't reason."
- Common Sense Media's 2026 teen survey found **38% of teens say AI reduces their own idea generation**.
- Kids who already struggle use AI more: **55% of teens who struggle with math use AI weekly for schoolwork**, versus 46% of those who find math manageable (Common Sense Media, 2026).

The pattern: the students with the biggest learning gaps are the most likely to cover them with AI.

## The 5-minute reasoning check (do this tonight)

Pick one finished homework problem or paragraph. Then:

1. **"Walk me through it."** Ask your child to explain the first step and why they chose it. Listen for reasons, not just a replay of the steps.
2. **Change the numbers.** For math, change one number and ask them to solve it again without notes. For reading, ask for a different piece of evidence from the same text.
3. **"What would go wrong if…?"** Ask what happens if they skipped a step or changed a word. Real understanding can predict errors.
4. **Say it in one sentence.** Ask for the main idea of their paragraph in one sentence, without looking.
5. **Watch for the same mistake.** If they make the same error on the new version, you've found a **mistake pattern** — the real blocker.

If your child can do 4 of 5 comfortably, the learning is probably there. If they stall on 2 or more, the homework is showing you the answer, not the understanding.

## How most programs miss this

Most homework help — human or AI — is built to get tonight's assignment finished. That feels productive, but it can hide the exact gap that shows up later on tests. More worksheets don't fix it either, if the child is repeating the same mistake pattern on every page.

- Does it ask your child to **explain**, or only to **answer**?

## FAQ

**Is it bad for my child to use AI for homework?**
Not always. AI can help with brainstorming or checking work. It becomes a problem when it replaces the thinking — when your child can't explain or repeat the work without it.

**How can I tell if my child actually understands their homework?**
Ask them to explain one step, then change a number or detail and have them redo it without help. If they can't, they understood the answer, not the concept.

**Why do grades look fine but test scores drop?**
Homework can be completed with help (from AI, apps, or adults). Tests show what a child can do alone. A gap between the two usually points to a hidden learning gap.

**Should I ban AI at home?**
Bans often don't work — in the 2026 Common Sense Media teen survey, 59% of teens whose school blocked AI simply switched to personal devices. Clear rules work better: AI can check work, but your child must be able to explain the answer.

**What age should parents start checking this?**
As soon as your child uses AI or search tools for schoolwork — often around grades 4–6 — and consistently through high school.
