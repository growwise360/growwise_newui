# Math Olympiad by Grade: Which Contest Fits Your Child?

Parents search "math olympiad for 3rd grade," "for 5th grade," "for middle school" — usually after a teacher says their child is "good at math" and they wonder what's next.

Competition math is different from school math. It's less about speed and more about **reasoning through problems you've never seen**. That's why a strong school math student isn't always ready — and why a child who is bored in class sometimes thrives.

## Which contest fits which grade

*Details below are from each organizer's published information as of September 2026. Always confirm dates and eligibility with the organizer or your testing site.*

| Contest | Who | Format | 2026–27 timing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MOEMS** (Math Olympiads for Elementary and Middle Schools) | Elementary division: grades 4–6; Middle division: grades 6–8 (younger students may enter) | 5 contests, 5 problems each, 30 minutes | Monthly, November–March |
| **AMC 8** (Mathematical Association of America) | Grade 8 and below, under 15.5 years old on test day | 25 multiple-choice questions, 40 minutes | **January 21–27, 2027** |
| **AMC 10 / AMC 12** (MAA) | AMC 10: grade 10 and below; AMC 12: grade 12 and below | 25 multiple-choice questions, 75 minutes | **A: Nov 5, 2026 · B: Nov 13, 2026** |

Students don't register for the AMC directly with MAA; they take it through an approved testing site such as a school or learning center.

## Signs your child may be ready

- Finishes school math quickly and asks "why does that work?"
- Enjoys puzzles and sticks with a hard problem for 10+ minutes.
- Can explain their reasoning out loud, not just give answers.
- Tries a different approach when the first one fails.

## Signs to build first

- Strong grades, but relies on memorized steps.
- Gets frustrated and stops when a problem looks unfamiliar.
- Makes the same **mistake pattern** under time pressure.
- Weak fraction, ratio or number sense despite good grades.

Competition prep works best on a solid foundation. For some children, the best "olympiad prep" is first closing a quiet gap in school math.

## The real problem behind the question

Many parents treat competitions as the goal. The real value is the thinking: **representing a problem, looking for patterns, testing cases and explaining why.** Those skills transfer to Algebra, Geometry and the SAT. Drilling past contests without building the reasoning usually stalls quickly.

## How most programs miss this

- Too much timed drilling, too little explanation.
- Contest tricks without the underlying concept.
- Placing children by grade instead of by reasoning level.

## FAQ

**What grade can kids start math olympiad?**
MOEMS offers an elementary division for grades 4–6, and younger students can sometimes participate. The AMC 8 is open to students in grade 8 and below.

**When is the AMC 8 in 2027?**
The 2027 AMC 8 is scheduled for January 21–27, 2027. Students take it through an approved testing site.

**What's the difference between MOEMS and AMC 8?**
MOEMS runs five short contests (5 problems, 30 minutes each) from November to March. The AMC 8 is a single 25-question, 40-minute multiple-choice contest in January.

**Is math olympiad good for kids who are already good at math?**
It can be, if they enjoy challenge and can explain their reasoning. Kids who rely on memorized steps may need to build conceptual understanding first.

**How do I prepare my child for math olympiad?**
Start with strategy-based problem solving and explaining solutions out loud, then add past contest problems. Make sure school math foundations are solid.
