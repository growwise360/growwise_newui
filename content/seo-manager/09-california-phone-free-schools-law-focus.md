# California's Phone-Free Schools Law: What It Means for Focus and Homework

This school year, many Tri-Valley parents are hearing a new set of rules about phones at school. Here's what changed, what it can and can't fix, and what to watch for at home.

## What the law requires

California's **Phone-Free Schools Act (AB 3216)**, signed in September 2024, requires school districts to **adopt policies limiting or prohibiting student smartphone use during the school day**. Districts had to adopt those policies **by July 1, 2026**, and must review them **at least once every five years**.

The law sets the requirement, but **each district writes its own policy**. Rules on where phones are stored, which parts of the day are covered (class, passing periods, lunch), and exceptions for emergencies or medical needs vary. Check your district's and school's current policy for the details that apply to your child.

## Common signs parents notice

- Your child finishes schoolwork faster at school but still stalls at home.
- Homework takes twice as long as it should, with frequent device checks.
- Your child says "I get it in class" but can't start the same work alone.
- Grades hold steady, but test scores slip.

## What may actually be happening

A phone rule removes one distraction at school. It doesn't remove it at home, where most homework and studying happen. And it doesn't fix a **learning gap**.

Often what looks like a focus problem is a **thinking gap**: the child avoids the work because they don't know how to start it. Checking the phone is the escape, not the cause. When the specific skill gap is closed, focus often improves on its own.

## What to do at home

1. **Match the school rule at home during homework.** Phone in another room for a set block, such as 25 minutes.
2. **Watch where they stop.** Note the exact problem or sentence where your child reaches for a device. That's usually where the gap is.
3. **Separate "won't" from "can't."** If they can explain the first step, it's a focus issue. If they can't, it's a skill issue.
4. **Check for a repeated mistake.** The same error across several problems is a **mistake pattern**, not carelessness.

## FAQ

**Are phones banned in California schools in 2026?**
California law (AB 3216) requires every district to adopt a policy limiting or prohibiting smartphone use during the school day, with policies due by July 1, 2026. Exact rules vary by district.

**When did California's phone-free school law take effect?**
Districts had to adopt their policies by July 1, 2026, so most new rules apply starting in the 2026–27 school year.

**Can my child still have a phone for emergencies?**
District policies typically include exceptions, such as emergencies or documented medical needs. Check your district's policy for details.

**Will a school phone ban improve my child's grades?**
It may reduce distraction during class, but it won't close existing learning gaps. If a skill is missing, targeted practice is still needed.

**Why can't my child focus on homework?**
Sometimes it's the device. Often it's avoidance: the child doesn't know how to start. Watch where they stop to find out which.
