# 12 Questions to Ask a Tutor Before You Hire One

Most parents choose a tutor based on price, schedule and a friendly first call. Those matter. But they don't tell you whether your child will actually learn.

These 12 questions do. Use them with any tutor or program — including us.

## Before you ask: know what you're solving

Write one sentence: *"My child is struggling with ___, and I notice it when ___."*
For example: "My child keeps losing points on math tests, and I notice it when problems have negative numbers."
That sentence helps any tutor show whether they can find the **real blocker**.

## Questions about diagnosis

**1. How will you find out exactly what my child is missing?**
Listen for: a specific assessment — not just "we'll look at the homework."
Red flag: starting lessons before any diagnostic.

**2. After the first session, what will you be able to tell me?**
Listen for: a named gap or **mistake pattern** (e.g., "sign errors when distributing").
Red flag: "They just need more practice."

**3. How do you tell a concept gap from a careless mistake?**
Listen for: checking with new problems and asking the child to explain.

## Questions about the plan

**4. What will a typical session look like?**
Listen for: a clear structure — review, targeted practice, independent check.

**5. Will sessions follow my child's school curriculum, fill gaps, or both?**
Listen for: both, with a reason tied to your child's needs.

**6. How often should my child come, and for how long?**
Listen for: a recommendation based on the gap. Research on effective tutoring points to regular sessions — often three or more a week — for students who are behind (Loeb, Stanford, 2022).

**7. What's the group size?**
Listen for: small enough for individual correction. Effective tutoring research generally points to groups of about three or fewer.

## Questions about the tutor

**8. Who will actually teach my child — and will it be the same person each week?**
Listen for: a consistent tutor. Consistency is one of the features linked to stronger results.

**9. What is your experience with this grade and subject?**
Listen for: specific experience, such as Integrated Math 1 or reading comprehension in middle school.

## Questions about progress

**10. How will I know it's working — and by when?**
Listen for: specific signs (fewer repeated errors, independent homework, test results) and a time frame for review.

**11. How often will you update me, and what will the update say?**
Listen for: short, specific updates about skills, not only attendance.

**12. What happens if it isn't working?**
Listen for: a plan to reassess and adjust.

## Red flags

- No diagnostic before starting
- Sessions spent only finishing tonight's homework
- Vague progress reports ("doing great!")
- Rotating tutors every week
- Long contracts before you've seen any results

## FAQ

**What questions should I ask a tutor before hiring?**
Ask how they'll diagnose your child's gap, what a session looks like, group size, who will teach, how progress is measured, and what happens if it isn't working.

**How do I choose a good tutor for my child?**
Pick one who starts with a real diagnostic, can name your child's specific gap, teaches in a small group or 1:1, stays consistent, and reports progress clearly.

**What are red flags when hiring a tutor?**
No diagnostic, homework-only sessions, vague progress reports, rotating tutors, and long contracts before results.

**How often should my child see a tutor?**
It depends on the gap. Research on high-impact tutoring points to regular sessions — often three or more times a week — for students who are behind.

**What should I ask an SAT tutor?**
Ask how they'll diagnose where points are lost, how they build a timeline around test dates, and how they track score changes on full practice tests.
