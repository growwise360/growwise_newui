# Is AI Weakening Your Child's Writing?

Many parents are noticing something odd: their child's essays look more polished than ever, yet their child can't explain what they wrote — or write a strong paragraph in class.

That isn't a coincidence. In a 2026 Common Sense Media survey of teens, **25% said they use AI output unchanged** and **31% said they rewrite AI output as their own**. **38% said AI reduces their own idea generation.**

Writing is where thinking becomes visible. When AI does the drafting, the thinking often gets skipped.

## The real problem behind the symptom

The symptom is "my child struggles to write." The real problem is usually one of three gaps:

1. **Idea gap** — the child doesn't know what they think yet, so they stall at the blank page.
2. **Evidence gap** — they make claims but can't connect them to specific proof from a text.
3. **Clarity gap** — they know what they mean, but their sentences don't carry it.

AI can cover all three. It gives ideas, supplies evidence, and smooths sentences. That's why the gap stays hidden until an in-class essay or timed test.

## What research and patterns show

- A January 2026 Brookings Institution report found a decline in critical thinking, "particularly in reading and writing skills," and noted that human essays contained **2 to 8 times more unique ideas** than ChatGPT outputs.
- Only **30% of teens** say teachers have discussed AI safety with them, and just **26%** received guidance on judging whether AI information is accurate (Common Sense Media, 2026).

Writing isn't only a school skill. It's how students show reasoning on every test, short answer, and application.

## 6 signs your child may be leaning on AI to write

1. The essay sounds more adult than how your child talks.
2. They can't summarize their own essay in one sentence.
3. In-class or timed writing is much weaker than homework writing.
4. They struggle to point to *where* in the text their evidence comes from.
5. Every paragraph has the same shape and rhythm.
6. They avoid writing a first draft by hand or without a device.

Two or more of these is worth a closer look — not as a discipline issue, but as a learning gap.

## How most programs miss this

Many writing programs grade the final product. If the final product is polished, the gap goes unnoticed. Others assign more essays, which gives more chances to lean on AI. Neither finds where the thinking breaks down.

## Why GrowWise?

- **Find the real blocker first** — idea, evidence, or clarity.
- **Reading with evidence** — students learn to locate and quote the exact line that supports a claim.
- **Short, visible writing** — one claim, one piece of evidence, one explanation, written in session, so the reasoning is on the page.
- **Explain it out loud** — if a student can say it clearly, they can write it clearly.
- **Targeted academic practice** on the specific pattern, not generic essay assignments.

## What to look for in any writing program

- Does it diagnose *which* part of writing is weak?
- Is writing done **in session**, not only as homework?
- Does it teach how to use evidence from a text?
- Does your child rewrite based on feedback, not start fresh each time?

## FAQ

**Is AI hurting my child's writing?**
It can if it replaces drafting and thinking. Using AI for feedback on a draft the child wrote is different from using it to write the draft.

**How can I tell if my child used AI to write an essay?**
Ask them to explain their main point and where their evidence came from. If they can't, the essay likely reflects the tool more than the student.

**Why can my child write at home but not in class?**
Home writing may involve AI, apps, or adult help. In-class writing shows independent skill. A big gap between them points to a writing gap.

**What is the fastest way to improve my child's writing?**
Short, frequent paragraphs with one claim, one piece of evidence, and one explanation — with feedback and a rewrite each time.

**What grade should kids write paragraphs independently?**
Expectations vary by school, but by upper elementary most students are asked to write organized paragraphs using text evidence. Check your child's grade-level standards with their teacher.
